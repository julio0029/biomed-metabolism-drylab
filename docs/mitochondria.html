The mitochondrial membrane.

NADH and succinate provide energy to a row of protein complexes. As energy moves along the row, protons are pumped. Their return through ATP synthase helps make ATP.

This is an overview, not a quantitative model. NADH enters at Complex I, succinate at Complex II, and oxygen is needed at the end of the chain.

Higher NADH can provide more energy to Complex I.

Succinate can provide energy through Complex II.

Low oxygen slows the final step and limits the whole chain.

Proton pumping: strong · ATP synthesis: supported

Energy from NADH and succinate can move through the complexes because oxygen is available at the end of the chain.
